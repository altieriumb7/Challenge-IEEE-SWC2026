1. Graph Topology: Delaunay Triangulation

Circumcircle Criterion:

\[ \forall \triangle ABC \in DT(P), \nexists D \in P \text{ inside the circumcircle of } \triangle ABC \]

Where \(P\) is the set of latitude/longitude coordinates for all intersections.

Topological Application:

To mathematically connect the CSV's isolated GPS coordinates into a realistic road network, we use Delaunay Triangulation. This specific algorithm maximizes the minimum angle of all triangles, preventing long, skinny (unrealistic) road connections. It perfectly reconstructs the planar mesh of the city's adjacency matrix in \(\mathcal{O}(V \log V)\) time, allowing the fluid dynamics to flow naturally between adjacent intersections.

2. The Physics: Webster HCM & Dynamic Phasing

\[ d = \frac{C(1-\lambda)^2}{2(1-\min(\lambda x, 0.99))} + \begin{cases} \frac{x^2}{2q_{sec}(1-x)} & x < 0.95 \\ 15 + 120(x - 0.95) & x \ge 0.95 \end{cases} \]

\(\lambda\) represents the Green Split (Directional Priority) dynamically assigned.

Dynamic Direction Reversal:

Traditional lights have fixed timers. Our Digital Twin features Dynamic Phasing: it actively changes the "direction" of the intersection's right-of-way by manipulating \(\lambda\) (the green time split) in real-time. If the AI routes a massive wave of trucks from the North, the equation dynamically stretches the North-South green cycle, effectively reversing the intersection's priority on the fly to absorb the shock.

3. The Routing: Spatial Water-filling

\[ \Delta v_i = - \phi \cdot (v_i - \bar{v}_{adj}) \cdot \frac{L_n}{\sum L_{adj}} \]

\(\phi\) is the routing coefficient. \(L_n\) represents the physical lanes of neighbor \(n\).

Example Application:

Node A has 1000 cars. Its neighbors (Node B: 4 lanes, Node C: 1 lane) average 500 cars. Node A needs to shed its excess. Because of the fractional multiplier \(L_n / \sum L_{adj}\), Node B will absorb 80% of the diverted cars, while the small 1-lane Node C will only absorb 20%. This prevents the algorithm from instantly causing gridlock in small alleys.

4. Computational Complexity

Day-use Scalability of the Digital Twin

  • Spatial Routing (Fluid Dynamics): \(\mathcal{O}(k \cdot V)\) where \(V\) is intersections and \(k\) is iterations (water-filling passes). Since the graph is planar, edges \(E \approx 3V\).
  • Webster HCM Calculation: \(\mathcal{O}(V)\) as delay is computed independently per node.
  • AI Inference (PPO Actor Network): \(\mathcal{O}(W)\) where \(W\) is the fixed number of weights in the neural network (~5,000 parameters). This is an \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) constant time operation per timestep.

Overall Pipeline Complexity: \(\mathcal{O}(V)\) Linear Time

Because the complexity scales linearly with the number of nodes rather than exponentially, the Digital Twin can be deployed on standard municipal hardware (without GPUs) and calculate the entire city's traffic state and AI optimal policy in under a millisecond per step.

Speech: "A model is useless if it can't run in real-time. What is the day-use complexity of our pipeline? The routing is linear relative to the intersections. The HCM calculation is linear. And the AI inference is a constant time operation, just a simple forward pass through a lightweight neural network. The entire pipeline scales in Big-O of V (Linear Time). You don't need a supercomputer to run this; the day-use Digital Twin runs locally on standard municipal hardware in milliseconds."
